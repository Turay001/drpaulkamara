Prof. Dr. Paul Kamara is the Chancellor and Chairman of the Board of Trustees of the University of Management and Technology (UNIMTECH), one of Sierra Leone's private universities, headquartered at Kissy Dockyard in Freetown. In UNIMTECH's own communications he is described as the institution's Proprietor — the founding figure behind an institution that began, in 1991, as a small computer training centre.

He shares this leadership role with the university's Pro-Chancellor, Dr. Paula Kamara. Together, university statements credit them with the "visionary leadership" that carried UNIMTECH from its origins through affiliation with Njala University, to full autonomy in 2021, and finally to permanent university status, granted by Sierra Leone's Tertiary Education Commission (TEC) in 2024.

02 — Profile

The man behind the institution

Who Prof. Dr. Paul Kamara is, told through what can be documented — with a clear line drawn between what is confirmed and what is not.

Public, verifiable information about Prof. Dr. Paul Kamara is closely tied to the institution he built. He is identified, consistently and by UNIMTECH's own official channels, as the university's Chancellor and Proprietor — titles that in Sierra Leone's private higher-education sector typically denote both ceremonial headship and a founding, ownership-level role in an institution's establishment and governance.

UNIMTECH's official account of its journey — from a small IT training centre founded in 1991 to a permanently accredited university in 2024 — is framed explicitly around his stewardship and that of Pro-Chancellor Dr. Paula Kamara. In August 2024, on the occasion of the university receiving permanent status, UNIMTECH's Acting Vice-Chancellor and Principal, Prof. Osman Sankoh, publicly thanked the Chancellor and Pro-Chancellor for the "visionary leadership" that had guided the institution to that milestone.

05 — Institutional Story

From a computer training centre to a university

UNIMTECH's own account of its 35-year journey, as published on its official website.

UNIMTECH's story begins on 16 July 1991, when it was founded in Freetown as the Kissy Computer Training Institute (KCTI) — a modest institution devoted to training computer users and IT professionals at a time when personal computing was only beginning to reach Sierra Leone.

As demand for information-technology skills grew through the 1990s and 2000s, the institution evolved in both scope and name — first into the Institute of Advanced Management and Technology (IAMTECH), and eventually into the University of Management and Technology (UNIMTECH). For part of its development, UNIMTECH operated in academic affiliation with Njala University, one of Sierra Leone's public universities, which the institution credits with shaping its academic standards during that period.

In 2021, UNIMTECH became a fully autonomous university, ending its affiliation with Njala. Three years later, in 2024, the Tertiary Education Commission (TEC) of Sierra Leone granted UNIMTECH permanent university status, effective 1 September 2024 — a milestone the university describes as the realisation of a vision that began in 1991.

10 — Leadership & Impact

Institution building, in his own university's words

The clearest documented evidence of Prof. Dr. Paul Kamara's impact is institutional rather than personal: UNIMTECH itself. Over more than three decades, an institution that began as a single computer training room in Freetown grew into a multi-campus, TEC-accredited university offering degrees across public administration, the social sciences, technology, and applied sciences.

UNIMTECH's leadership has publicly and repeatedly credited the Chancellor and Pro-Chancellor's stewardship for this trajectory — most notably at the point of permanent accreditation in 2024, and earlier at the university's 30th-anniversary observances in 2021, where the university chose to mark his and the Pro-Chancellor's contribution with a permanent statue on campus.

12 — Vision

Education, technology, and national development

UNIMTECH describes its mission as preparing graduates equipped to compete favourably in a dynamic labour market, and to contribute meaningfully to national and international development — with an ambition, stated on its own channels, to become Sierra Leone's leading university in management and technology.

That mission is anchored in the university's motto, "For Country and For Humanity", and in its stated commitment to making quality tertiary education available and affordable — including, explicitly, to disadvantaged populations, in support of national development goals.
